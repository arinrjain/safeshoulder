# Bullying Response Guide: Recognizing, Responding, and Recovering

## What is Bullying?

Bullying is **repeated, intentional harm** with a power imbalance. It's not a one-time conflict—it's a pattern.

**Bullying can be:**
- **Physical:** Hitting, pushing, damaging property
- **Verbal:** Name-calling, insults, threats, mocking
- **Social:** Exclusion, rumors, humiliation, isolation
- **Cyberbullying:** Online harassment, mean texts, hurtful posts

**It's NOT bullying if:**
- It happened once
- Both people have equal power
- It was unintentional
- It's a conflict between equals (not targeted harassment)

---

## Signs You're Being Bullied

### Emotional Signs
- Anxiety about school/work/social situations
- Depression or hopelessness
- Low self-esteem ("I deserve this," "I'm the problem")
- Shame or embarrassment
- Anger or rage
- Feeling alone or isolated
- Suicidal thoughts

### Physical Signs
- Stomachaches, headaches
- Sleep problems (too much or too little)
- Loss of appetite or overeating
- Not wanting to go to school/work
- Injuries you can't explain

### Behavioral Signs
- Withdrawing from friends and activities
- Avoiding certain people or places
- Change in grades or performance
- Becoming aggressive or acting out
- Using substances to cope
- Self-harm

### Social Signs
- Losing friends
- Being excluded from groups
- Rumors about you
- Being laughed at or mocked

---

## What to Do RIGHT NOW

### If You're In Immediate Danger

**Call 911 or get to safety immediately.**

- Leave the situation
- Go to a trusted adult
- Don't try to handle it alone

### If It Just Happened (Within Hours)

**1. Get to safety**
- Remove yourself from the bullying situation
- Go to a trusted adult, teacher, counselor, or parent

**2. Tell someone**
- Parent, teacher, counselor, school administrator
- Be specific: "This is what happened, when, and who was involved"
- Don't minimize it or blame yourself

**3. Don't respond or retaliate**
- Bullies want a reaction—don't give them one
- Responding usually escalates the situation
- Screenshot/save evidence if it's online

**4. Document it**
- Write down what happened, when, who was there
- Keep screenshots of cyberbullying
- Save texts, emails, posts
- This helps adults intervene

## Responding to Bullying: Short-Term Strategies

### If You're Being Bullied in the Moment

**Your safety comes first.** Pick the strategy that works for your situation:

#### Strategy 1: The "Boring" Response
Bullies want a reaction. Give them nothing.

- **They say:** "You're so stupid"
- **You say:** "Okay" (flat tone, no emotion)
- **Then:** Walk away

Don't argue, defend, or show upset. Boring responses don't reward the bully.

#### Strategy 2: The Confident Response
Act unaffected (even if you're scared inside).

- **They say:** "Nobody likes you"
- **You say:** "That's not true" (confident tone)
- **Then:** Walk away with your head up

**Key:** Your body language matters. Don't look scared or angry.

#### Strategy 3: Humor (Only if you're comfortable)
Deflect with humor—but only if it feels safe.

- **They say:** "Your outfit is ugly"
- **You say:** "Yeah, I woke up and thought, 'How can I look worse today?'" (laugh it off)
- **Then:** Move on

**Only use this if:** You feel safe and it doesn't escalate. Stop immediately if the bully gets meaner.

#### Strategy 4: Assertive Response
Name the behavior clearly and set a boundary.

- **They say:** "You're such a loser"
- **You say:** "Stop talking to me like that. I don't deserve to be treated this way."
- **Then:** Walk away (don't wait for their response)

---

### When to Tell an Adult

**Tell a trusted adult if:**
- It's happening repeatedly
- You feel unsafe
- The bully won't stop
- It's affecting your grades, sleep, or mental health
- You're considering self-harm

**Trusted adults:**
- Parent or guardian
- School counselor
- Teacher
- Coach
- Family member
- School administrator

**What to say:**
- "I'm being bullied and I need help"
- Be specific about what's happening
- Name the people involved
- Say how it's affecting you
- Ask them what they're going to do about it

## Responding to Cyberbullying

Cyberbullying is bullying through texts, social media, gaming, or online platforms.

### Immediate Actions

1. **Don't respond or engage**
   - Cyberbullies feed on reactions
   - Your response is often what they want

2. **Screenshot everything**
   - Evidence is important
   - Save the date, time, and context

3. **Block the person**
   - Most platforms have block features
   - Block doesn't stop them from seeing, but they can't contact you

4. **Report the content**
   - Most platforms let you report bullying
   - Click "Report abuse" or similar
   - Platforms remove content that violates policies

5. **Tell a trusted adult**
   - Show them the screenshots
   - Let them know how it's affecting you
   - Ask for help deciding next steps

### Longer-Term Solutions

- **Change privacy settings** - Limit who can see your posts
- **Don't post personal information** - Address, phone, schedule
- **Be selective about friends** - Only add people you know
- **Think before posting** - Would you want this said about you?
- **Don't forward mean posts** - You're spreading the bullying

## Managing the Emotional Impact

### In the Short Term

**When anxiety hits:**
- Use grounding: 5-4-3-2-1 technique (5 things you see, 4 you touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste)
- Deep breathing: In for 4, hold for 4, out for 4
- Move your body: Walk, stretch, dance

**When shame feels overwhelming:**
- Remember: This is NOT your fault
- You didn't cause this by being "different" or "wrong"
- The bully has a problem; you don't deserve this

**When you feel isolated:**
- Tell one trusted person
- Spend time with people who value you
- Find one supportive activity or group

### Processing What Happened

**Talk to someone:**
- A counselor or therapist
- A trusted friend
- A family member
- A crisis line if you're having dark thoughts

**What to expect:**
- You might feel angry at yourself ("Why didn't I stand up for myself?")
- You might replay moments ("I should have said...")
- This is normal. Talking helps process it.

**Therapy can help with:**
- Processing the hurt
- Rebuilding self-esteem
- Managing anxiety
- Deciding how to move forward
- Figuring out if you want to return to that school/group/job

## Rebuilding Your Confidence

### Step 1: Separate Bullying From Truth

**Bullies say things that hurt, but that doesn't make them true.**

- Bully: "You're ugly"
- Truth: "I'm attractive to people who matter to me"

- Bully: "Nobody likes you"
- Truth: "I have real friends who value me"

- Bully: "You're too weird"
- Truth: "I'm unique, and that's a strength"

**Exercise:** For each mean thing they said, write the truth. Keep it visible.

### Step 2: Reconnect With Your People

**Bullying isolates you. Rebuild connection:**

- Spend time with people who treat you well
- Join a club, team, or group you enjoy
- Do activities where you feel competent
- Find communities (online or in-person) with your interests

### Step 3: Do Things That Make You Feel Good

- Hobbies you enjoy
- Physical activity
- Time in nature
- Creative outlets
- Helping others
- Learning something new

### Step 4: Practice Self-Compassion

- Treat yourself like you'd treat a hurt friend
- Notice your inner critic ("I'm weak," "I caused this")
- Replace with: "This happened to me. I'm handling it. I'm going to be okay."

## When to Leave the Situation

### Consider changing schools/jobs/groups if:

- Adults won't intervene or don't believe you
- Bullying is escalating despite reporting
- The environment is unsafe
- Your mental health is deteriorating
- You're having thoughts of suicide
- You've tried multiple strategies and nothing changes

**This isn't giving up. This is protecting yourself.**

Many people who changed schools/jobs say it was the best decision they made.

---

## Supporting Someone Else Who's Being Bullied

### If a friend is being bullied:

- **Believe them** - Don't minimize ("It's not that bad")
- **Listen without judgment** - Let them share their experience
- **Tell an adult** - Don't try to handle it alone as a peer
- **Stay their friend** - Bullies isolate victims; your friendship matters
- **Don't gang up** - Don't get friends to confront the bully (can escalate)
- **Include them** - Invite them to things, make sure they feel valued

## Crisis Resources

**If you're having thoughts of self-harm or suicide:**
- Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741
- 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline: Call or text 988
- NAMI Helpline: 1-800-950-NAMI

**If you're in immediate danger:**
- Call 911
- Go to nearest emergency room

---

## Remember

1. **It's not your fault.** Bullying says something about the bully, not about you.

2. **You're not alone.** Many people have been bullied and recovered fully.

3. **It can get better.** With support and time, the pain lessens.

4. **Your life extends beyond this.** School, jobs, and friend groups are temporary. Your worth is permanent.

5. **Speaking up is brave.** Telling an adult isn't "snitching"—it's taking care of yourself.
