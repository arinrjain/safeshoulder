# Workplace Discrimination and Harassment: Your Rights and Next Steps

## What is Workplace Discrimination?

**Discrimination** is being treated unfairly based on a protected characteristic:
- Race/ethnicity
- Gender/gender identity
- Age
- Disability
- Sexual orientation
- Religion
- National origin
- Pregnancy
- Genetic information

**It's illegal.** Federal law protects you (Title VII of Civil Rights Act).

---

## What is Harassment?

**Harassment** is unwelcome conduct based on protected characteristic that:
- Creates hostile work environment
- Is severe or pervasive
- Affects your ability to work
- Includes slurs, jokes, comments, exclusion

**Examples:**
- Racist jokes or slurs
- Sexual comments or touching
- Mocking your disability
- Asking invasive questions about immigration status
- Assumptions based on gender/race
- Being excluded from opportunities because of identity
- "You're so articulate for a [race]"
- Comments about aging out of job
- Pregnancy discrimination

**It's illegal.** But it happens constantly.

---

## Why It Happens

**Discrimination happens because:**
- People have biases (conscious or unconscious)
- Companies don't enforce policies
- Harasser faces no consequences
- Targets are isolated or vulnerable
- Power differential (boss, dominant group)
- Company culture tolerates it

**It's not your fault. It's not about you. It's about systemic discrimination.**

## Impact on You

### Psychological
- Anxiety about going to work
- Depression
- Shame (internalizing that something's wrong with you)
- Hypervigilance (always alert)
- PTSD symptoms

### Physical
- Sleep disruption
- Stress-related illness
- Headaches
- Stomach issues
- Chronic pain

### Professional
- Can't focus on work
- Performance decline
- Missing work due to stress
- Avoiding certain areas/people
- Considering quitting

### Financial
- Potential job loss
- Legal costs
- Therapy costs
- Impact on references

**This is a real harm with real consequences.**

## If It's Happening to You: Immediate Steps

### Document Everything

**Keep a record:**
- Date and time
- What happened (specific words/actions)
- Who was involved (witnesses?)
- How it made you feel
- Impact (couldn't work, left early, etc.)
- Your response

**Keep in:**
- Personal journal (not company system)
- Notes app on personal phone
- Email to yourself

**Why:** Proves pattern. One incident might be dismissed; pattern proves it.

### Don't Ignore It or Minimize It

**Temptation:** "Maybe I'm overreacting. Maybe it's just a joke."

**Reality:** If it bothers you, it's not okay. You don't need permission to feel upset.

### Tell Someone

**Options:**
1. **Talk to HR** (risky but official)
2. **Tell your manager** (if they're not the harasser)
3. **Tell trusted coworker** (builds witness/support)
4. **Contact employment lawyer** (before HR if unsure)

**If you go to HR:**
- Be factual and specific
- Have documentation
- Know you might face retaliation (illegal but happens)
- Follow up in writing
- Keep copies of everything

---

## Reporting to HR

### What to Say

"I've been experiencing [discrimination/harassment] based on my [protected characteristic]. This includes [specific incident]. I need this addressed. Here's my documentation."

### What HR Should Do

- Take complaint seriously
- Investigate (talk to witnesses, harasser)
- Protect you from retaliation
- Discipline harasser appropriately
- Monitor to prevent recurrence

### What Often Happens Instead

- Minimizes your complaint
- Blames you ("You're too sensitive")
- Makes situation worse
- Retaliates (or allows it)
- Doesn't investigate properly
- "Sides with" the harasser

**If this happens, it's additional illegal behavior (retaliation).**

## Legal Options

### Option 1: EEOC Complaint (Free)

**What it is:**
- Equal Employment Opportunity Commission
- Federal agency that investigates discrimination
- Free

**How to file:**
- Go to EEOC.gov
- File online or by mail
- Must file within 180-300 days (varies by state)
- You'll be assigned investigator

**What happens:**
- EEOC investigates
- Issues findings
- Notifies you of rights
- Sends "right to sue" letter
- You can then sue if you want

**Pros:** Free, federal backing, doesn't require lawyer initially
**Cons:** Slow, EEOC sometimes sides with employer, limited outcomes

### Option 2: Employment Lawyer

**What it is:**
- Private attorney who handles employment law
- Works on contingency (no upfront cost, takes % of settlement)

**How to find:**
- Bar association referral
- Online (Avvo, FindLaw)
- Local law school clinic
- Legal aid (free if low-income)

**What they do:**
- Evaluate your case
- File complaints (EEOC and/or court)
- Negotiate settlement
- Represent you in litigation

**Pros:** Professional help, might get damages, pressure on employer
**Cons:** Takes time, uncertainty, stressful

### Option 3: Both (Recommended)

File EEOC complaint (free) AND hire lawyer (contingency). Covers bases.

## Retaliation: Your Legal Protection

**It's illegal for employer to retaliate against you for:**
- Filing complaint
- Talking about discrimination
- Cooperating with investigation
- Refusing illegal request

**Retaliation includes:**
- Firing
- Demotion
- Pay cut
- Worse assignments
- Isolation
- Written warnings
- Anything negative after you reported

**If retaliation happens, that's ANOTHER illegal act. Document it.**

## Staying Safe While Employed

### Set Boundaries

- Document everything (already doing this)
- Keep conversations professional
- Minimize one-on-one time with harasser
- Have witnesses present when possible
- Don't engage in friendly banter (keep distance)

### Build Your Case

- Continue documenting
- Keep copies at home (not work computer)
- Save all emails
- Record conversations (if legal in your state)
- Note impact (doctor visits, therapy, sick days)
- Get witness statements if willing

### Protect Your Mental Health

- Therapy (someone trained in workplace trauma)
- Support group (others experiencing discrimination)
- Connection with community
- Self-care (you need it)

### Know Your Exit Plan

- Could you leave if needed?
- Do you have financial cushion?
- Is job worth your mental health?
- What's your timeline?

## Should You Leave or Fight?

### Reasons to Consider Leaving

- Harassment is severe
- Company won't address it
- Retaliation is happening
- Your mental health is deteriorating
- You don't think you'll win
- You want to move on

**Leaving is valid.** You don't have to fight.

### Reasons to Consider Fighting

- Discrimination is clear
- You have documentation
- You believe company will address it
- You want to set precedent
- Financial settlement would help
- You have legal backing

**Fighting is also valid.** Your choice.

### Both Approaches Have Value

**Leaving:**
- Removes yourself from harm
- Lets you heal
- You get a fresh start
- You don't have to relive it in legal proceedings

**Fighting:**
- Holds company accountable
- Prevents harm to others
- Possible financial settlement
- Sets precedent
- Can be empowering

**Choose what's right for YOU, not what's "right" in general.**
