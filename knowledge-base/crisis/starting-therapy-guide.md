# Starting Therapy: What to Expect and How to Get the Most Out of It

## Why Therapy Matters

**Therapy is:**
- Not weakness (it's strength)
- Not just for "crazy" people (it's for anyone struggling)
- Not a permanent commitment (you can stop anytime)
- Not a quick fix (healing takes time, but it works)
- One of the best investments you can make

**Therapy helps with:**
- Processing past trauma
- Managing current struggles
- Building coping skills
- Understanding yourself better
- Changing unhelpful patterns
- Healing relationships
- Finding meaning and direction

---

## Finding a Therapist

### Where to Look

**Insurance:** Call your insurance, get in-network therapists
**Psychology Today:** psychologytoday.com (search by specialty, insurance)
**LGBTQ+ Therapists:** therapyforpride.com, lgbtqhealth.org
**Sliding Scale:** Open Path Collective, SAMHSA locator (free/cheap)
**Crisis Appointments:** Most therapists have emergency slots

### What to Look For

**Ideal therapist:**
- Trained in relevant modality (CBT for anxiety, DBT for self-harm, etc.)
- Experienced with your issues
- Good fit (you feel comfortable)
- Licensed (LCSW, Ph.D, M.D., etc. - not just "counselor")
- Takes your insurance or affordable
- Available at times you need

**Red flags:**
- ❌ Doesn't listen
- ❌ Pushes specific agenda (religion, politics)
- ❌ Oversteps boundaries
- ❌ Doesn't follow up on what you say
- ❌ Makes you feel worse
- ❌ No clear treatment plan

### How to Start

1. **Call/email** saying you're looking for therapy
2. **Ask about:**
   - Do they take your insurance?
   - Do they have openings?
   - What's their approach?
   - Can you do a brief call first?
3. **Schedule first appointment**
4. **Go prepared** (see below)

## Your First Appointment

### What Happens

**Intake (30-45 min):**
- They ask questions about your history
- Mental health history
- Current issues
- Medications
- Insurance info
- Legal stuff (confidentiality, emergency contacts)

**Therapy (remaining time):**
- You talk about what brought you
- They listen and ask questions
- They might give feedback or explanation
- You might feel emotional

### What to Bring

- Insurance card
- ID
- List of current medications (if any)
- Emergency contact
- Payment method (if not insurance)

### What to Talk About

**Be honest about:**
- What's wrong
- How long it's been happening
- What you've tried
- How it affects your life
- Your goals for therapy
- Past trauma/struggles
- Current support system

**Don't:**
- ❌ Minimize (therapist needs real picture)
- ❌ Perform (you're not being judged)
- ❌ Hide things (secrets limit help)
- ❌ Be perfect (bring your messy self)

## The First Few Sessions

### What to Expect

- Sessions 1-3 are mostly listening (they're assessing)
- You'll feel awkward (normal)
- You might cry (very normal)
- They might ask hard questions
- You might not see progress yet
- You're building relationship

### Is It the Right Therapist?

**By session 3, ask yourself:**
- Do I feel heard?
- Do I trust them?
- Do they seem competent?
- Am I comfortable?
- Do I want to continue?

**If not, try a different therapist.** Fit matters.

### Common First Session Feelings

- Relief (finally talking to someone)
- Sadness (emotional wound opened)
- Nothing (sometimes you're numb)
- Doubt (Did I do the right thing?)
- Hope (Maybe things can change)

**All normal. Stick with it.**

## Making Therapy Work

### Come Consistently

- Same day/time if possible
- Don't skip (breaks progress)
- Even when you don't feel like it
- Healing isn't linear (some weeks harder than others)

### Be Honest

- Tell them about uncomfortable topics
- Tell them if something they said didn't land
- Tell them if you want to try something different
- Vulnerability is the whole point

### Do the Homework

- Most therapists assign "homework"
- Worksheets, journaling, exposure tasks, etc.
- This is where real change happens
- In-session is 50 min. Outside session is 10,000 min.

### Tell Them About Progress

- Small wins matter
- Celebrate what's working
- It keeps you motivated
- It guides the treatment

## Common Therapy Approaches (Brief Overview)

### Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)

**What it is:** Change thoughts → change feelings → change behavior

**Good for:** Anxiety, depression, OCD, specific fears

**How it works:**
- Identify unhelpful thoughts
- Challenge them with evidence
- Practice new behaviors
- Build skills

### Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT)

**What it is:** Accept reality + commit to change

**Good for:** Suicidal thoughts, self-harm, emotional dysregulation, borderline traits

**How it works:**
- Skills training (distress tolerance, emotion regulation, mindfulness)
- Weekly individual therapy
- Phone coaching
- Therapist team consultation

### Psychodynamic/Psychoanalytic

**What it is:** Explore past patterns, understand why you do what you do

**Good for:** Long-standing patterns, relationship issues, trauma, identity

**How it works:**
- Talk freely (sometimes "free association")
- Therapist looks for patterns
- Explore childhood/past
- Slow but deep

### Somatic/Trauma-Focused

**What it is:** Healing trauma stored in body

**Good for:** PTSD, complex trauma, abuse survivors

**How it works:**
- Process trauma memories
- Body-based techniques (EMDR, Somatic Experiencing)
- Release trauma from nervous system
- Restore sense of safety

### Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT)

**What it is:** Accept what you can't change, commit to values

**Good for:** Anxiety, chronic pain, grief, any persistent struggle

**How it works:**
- Identify your values
- Accept difficult feelings
- Act in line with values anyway
- Build meaningful life

## Medication and Therapy

### Should You Take Medication?

**Factors:**
- Severity of symptoms
- How long it's lasted
- If therapy alone is working
- Your preference
- Your therapist's recommendation

**Common medications:**
- Antidepressants (help depression, anxiety, sometimes OCD)
- Anti-anxiety (fast-acting, short-term)
- Mood stabilizers (bipolar, mood dysregulation)
- Antipsychotics (psychosis, sometimes anxiety)

**Therapy + medication > medication or therapy alone** (for many people)

### How to Talk to Your Doctor

- Tell them what you're struggling with
- Say what you want (medication, therapy, both)
- Ask about side effects
- It takes time to find right medication (4-6 weeks often)
- Dose adjustments are normal

## Dealing with Therapy Challenges

### What If You're Not Making Progress?

**Options:**
1. **Give it more time** (sometimes 10+ sessions to see change)
2. **Adjust the approach** ("Can we try something different?")
3. **Try different therapist** (fit might be wrong)
4. **Assess outside therapy** (Are you doing the work? Meds helping?)

### What If You Feel Worse?

**Normal reasons:**
- Bringing up painful stuff (processing it)
- Feeling emotions you've been numb to
- Confronting reality
- This is often how healing starts

**When to be concerned:**
- If you're having thoughts of self-harm
- If you're unable to function
- If your symptoms are getting worse (not just uncomfortable)
- Tell your therapist immediately

### What If You Want to Quit?

**Common reasons:**
- It's hard (therapy is work)
- You think you're fine (you're not)
- Therapist is wrong (maybe, check if true)
- You're ashamed (don't be)
- Cost is too much (ask about sliding scale)

**Before you quit:**
- Talk to your therapist about it
- See if something needs to change
- Give yourself a deadline to reassess
- Sometimes quitting is right (wrong therapist)
- Sometimes pushing through is right (the hard work pays off)

## Confidentiality and Limits

### What's Confidential

- Everything you say in therapy
- Therapist can't tell anyone
- Exception: Client requests release

### Limits to Confidentiality

Therapist must break confidentiality if:
- You're in immediate danger (suicidal with plan)
- Someone else is in danger (you'll hurt someone)
- Child abuse or neglect
- Elder abuse
- Court order

**Know these limits. Ask your therapist about their specific policies.**

## How Long Does Therapy Take?

### Short-term (6-20 sessions)

- Specific problem (phobia, public speaking anxiety)
- Crisis support (getting through immediate crisis)
- Life coaching
- Skills building

### Medium-term (20-50 sessions)

- Depression, anxiety
- Relationship issues
- Specific traumas
- Building new patterns

### Long-term (50+ sessions)

- Complex trauma
- Personality issues
- Deep-seated patterns
- Long-standing mental health conditions
- Ongoing support for chronic conditions

**Duration depends on:**
- Issue complexity
- Your readiness to change
- Life circumstances
- Therapy approach
- Your goals

## Getting the Most Out of Therapy

### Between Sessions

- Do the homework
- Journal about insights
- Notice patterns
- Celebrate small wins
- Practice skills learned
- Read recommended books

### In Session

- Bring your actual life (not polished version)
- Tell them what's not working
- Ask questions if confused
- Share breakthroughs
- Discuss struggles with homework
- Be vulnerable

### After Therapy (When You're Done)

- You don't have to be "cured" (you just have tools)
- You can return anytime (therapy isn't one-time)
- You'll still have hard days (normal)
- You'll have skills to handle them
- You're stronger for having done the work

## When to Seek Emergency Help

**Go to ER or call 988 if:**
- You're having thoughts of self-harm
- You're having suicidal thoughts with a plan
- You're hearing voices/seeing things
- You're unable to care for yourself
- You're a danger to others
- You can't function at all

**Don't wait for therapy appointment. Get immediate help.**

## Cost Concerns

### If You Can't Afford It

- **Sliding scale:** Therapist charges based on income
- **Community centers:** Often free or low-cost
- **Open Path Collective:** Sessions for $30
- **SAMHSA:** Free mental health treatment locator
- **Crisis lines:** Free and immediate
- **Support groups:** Free peer support
- **Universities:** Psychology graduate students (supervised, cheap)

**There are options. Reach out.**

## Remember

1. **Asking for help is strength.** Takes courage.

2. **Therapy works.** Research proves it (especially with consistency).

3. **It's not about the therapist "fixing" you.** It's about you finding your own answers.

4. **You might need to try a few therapists.** Fit matters.

5. **Healing isn't linear.** Some weeks are harder. That's normal.

6. **You deserve to feel better.** Therapy is one path there.
