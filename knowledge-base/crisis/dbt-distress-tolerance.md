# DBT Distress Tolerance Skills: Crisis Survival Techniques

## What is Distress Tolerance?

Distress tolerance is about surviving crises and tolerating pain without making things worse. Unlike emotion regulation (changing how you feel), distress tolerance is about getting through difficult moments without self-harm or destructive behaviors.

**Goal:** Accept reality and tolerate emotional pain in the moment.

## When to Use Distress Tolerance Skills

- You're in immediate crisis or acute emotional pain
- You can't change the situation right now
- You're tempted to engage in harmful coping (self-harm, substance use, reckless behavior)
- You need to survive a moment until the crisis passes

## The TIPP Skill: Physical Body Change

When emotions are overwhelming, changing your physical state can reset your nervous system.

### T - **Temperature**

Splash your face with **very cold water** (or hold ice against your face for 30 seconds). This triggers the "dive response" and can break panic cycles.

- **Why it works:** The sudden cold activates your parasympathetic nervous system
- **How:** Use water from the fridge, ice, or even cold air
- **When:** When you're panicking, having intrusive thoughts, or in acute distress
- **Caution:** Not for those with heart conditions—check with a doctor first

### I - **Intense Exercise**

Do intense physical activity for even 1-2 minutes to burn off stress chemicals.

- Run up and down stairs
- Do jumping jacks
- Sprint
- Dance intensely

- **Why it works:** Burns off adrenaline and raises endorphins
- **How long:** Even 60 seconds can help
- **Bonus:** Gives you something to do with your hands/body

### P - **Paced Breathing**

Slow down your breathing to calm your nervous system.

- **Technique:** Breathe in for 4 counts, hold for 4, out for 4
- **Or:** Breathe in for 4, out for 6 (longer exhales calm the system)
- **Duration:** 2-5 minutes minimum for effects
- **Why it works:** Deep breathing activates your calming (parasympathetic) nervous system

### P - **Paired Muscle Relaxation**

Tense and release muscle groups while breathing slowly.

1. Tense a muscle group for 5 seconds
2. Release and breathe out slowly
3. Move through your body: fists, arms, shoulders, face, abs, legs

- **Why it works:** Reduces physical tension that fuels emotional distress

## The DISTRESS Skill: Distracting Yourself

When you can't change the situation, distraction can help you survive the moment.

### D - **Distracting Activities**

Pick an activity that **fully engages your mind** (not just scrolling):

- **Sensory:** Listen to music, look at art, smell a candle
- **Mental:** Solve a puzzle, play a game, read, write
- **Physical:** Clean, exercise, sports, dancing
- **Social:** Call a friend, go to a group activity
- **Other:** Cook, craft, gardening, building something

**Key:** Pick something engaging enough to interrupt rumination.

### I - **Improving the Moment**

Small actions that make right now slightly better:

- Make tea or hot chocolate
- Light a candle
- Put on your favorite song
- Wrap in a blanket
- Take a warm shower
- Go for a walk
- Pet an animal
- Look at photos

These don't solve the problem, but they make the current moment more tolerable.

### S - **Self-Soothing (The Five Senses)**

Comfort yourself through your senses:

- **Vision:** Watch a beautiful video, look at art, go outside
- **Sound:** Listen to calming music, nature sounds, podcasts
- **Smell:** Light a candle, smell essential oils, coffee
- **Taste:** Slowly eat something you enjoy—focus on the taste
- **Touch:** Warm blanket, soft clothing, pet an animal, soft textures

Slow down and really experience each sensation.

### T - **Thinking of Others**

Shift perspective:

- Think of people who care about you
- Remember times you've survived before
- Imagine what a mentor would say
- Help someone else
- Acts of kindness for others

### R - **Radical Acceptance**

Accept reality as it is right now, without judgment.

**What it's NOT:**
- Giving up
- Being okay with it
- Liking the situation

**What it IS:**
- Acknowledging the reality of the situation
- Reducing suffering from fighting reality
- Creating space to respond rather than react

**Practice:**
- "This is what's happening right now."
- "I cannot change this moment, but I can survive it."
- "Accepting this doesn't mean I accept it forever—just right now."

### E - **Evaluate Pros and Cons**

List the advantages and disadvantages of tolerating the crisis vs. acting on destructive urges.

**Tolerating the pain:**
- Pro: I survive, things improve, I feel proud
- Con: It hurts right now

**Acting destructively (self-harm, substance use, etc):**
- Pro: Momentary relief
- Con: Makes things worse, adds guilt, blocks real solutions

### S - **Shielding/Soothing**

Protect yourself from getting worse:

- Avoid triggers if possible
- Stay with trusted people
- Avoid alcohol/drugs
- Get basic care (food, water, sleep)
- Do one thing that makes you feel safer

## Distress Tolerance Checklist

When you're in crisis:

- [ ] **TIPP** - Try one physical reset technique
- [ ] **Distract** - Engage your mind fully
- [ ] **Improve the moment** - Make now slightly better
- [ ] **Self-soothe** - Comfort through senses
- [ ] **Radical acceptance** - "This is what is. I can survive this."
- [ ] **Reach out** - Text a friend, call a crisis line
- [ ] **Wait it out** - Most intense emotions peak and pass

## Remember

Distress tolerance isn't about:
- Never feeling bad
- Never being in crisis
- "Toughening up"

It's about:
- **Surviving** difficult moments
- **Not making it worse** while you're vulnerable
- **Getting through** until you can problem-solve

**Crisis Lines:**
- Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741
- National Suicide Prevention Lifeline: 988
- SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357

---

*These skills are based on Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) research. Practice them when calm so they're easier to use in crisis.*
