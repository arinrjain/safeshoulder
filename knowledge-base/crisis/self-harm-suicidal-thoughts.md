# Self-Harm and Suicidal Thoughts: Crisis Support

## If You're in Immediate Danger

**STOP. CALL 911 or go to an emergency room NOW.**

You don't have to feel like this. Help is available right now.

**National Suicide Prevention Lifeline: 988**
- Call or text 988 (available 24/7)
- Free, confidential, trained counselors
- They've helped millions

**Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741**
- Text anytime, anywhere
- Immediate response

**International Association for Suicide Prevention: https://www.iasp.info/resources/Crisis_Centres/**

## What You're Feeling Right Now

### If You're Having Suicidal Thoughts

**These are real thoughts, but they're not the whole truth.**

What your brain is saying:
- "Everyone would be better off without me"
- "This pain will never end"
- "I'm a burden"
- "There's no way out"
- "I should hurt myself"

**What's actually true:**
- Your pain is temporary (even though it doesn't feel that way)
- There ARE ways out that don't involve dying
- People DO care about you (even if you can't feel it)
- This is a crisis moment, not your permanent state
- The fact that you're reading this means part of you wants to survive

### If You're Hurting Yourself

**Self-harm might feel like:**
- Relief (temporary numbness or control)
- Punishment (you think you deserve it)
- Communication (expressing pain you can't say)
- Escape (breaking mental pain temporarily)

**But it's not actually solving the problem.** It's a coping mechanism that provides temporary relief at the cost of your safety.

## Understanding Why You Feel This Way

### Suicidal thoughts often come from:

**Unbearable emotional pain**
- Overwhelming grief
- Intense shame or guilt
- Feeling trapped with no way out
- Loss of someone/something important
- Years of accumulated suffering

**Brain chemistry**
- Depression alters how your brain processes pain
- Everything feels worse and more hopeless
- Your brain isn't thinking clearly—it's in survival mode

**Crisis moment**
- Breakup, job loss, rejection
- Confrontation or betrayal
- Trigger anniversary
- Accumulation of stress

**Important:** If you're having these thoughts, it's not because you're weak or broken. It's because your pain exceeds your current coping resources. That's fixable.

---

## Why Suicide Isn't the Answer (Even If It Feels Like It)

### The Permanence Problem

Suicidal thoughts are temporary. Suicide is permanent.

- The crisis that feels unbearable today will feel different in a week
- Your perspective will shift when you're not in acute pain
- New opportunities will appear you can't see now
- People you haven't met yet will matter to you

**Example:** 90% of people who survive suicide attempts are grateful they survived. They say "I didn't want to die, I wanted the pain to stop."

### What Suicide Actually Does

- **For you:** Ends possibilities (not pain—just your future)
- **For others:** Creates permanent trauma for everyone who knew you
- **Impact:** Parents/siblings/friends experience decades of "why" and guilt

### Better Options Exist

- Hospitalization (getting through the crisis)
- Medication (can dramatically change how you feel)
- Therapy (processing the underlying pain)
- Crisis hotlines (immediate support)
- Medication changes (if current meds aren't working)
- Time (it does heal, even though it's slow)

## If You're Thinking About Self-Harm

### Why Your Brain Suggests It

Self-harm feels like it works because:
- It converts emotional pain into physical pain (feels more manageable)
- It provides relief/numbness (temporarily)
- It feels like you have control
- It communicates pain you can't express verbally

**But the cycle:**
1. Emotional pain builds
2. Self-harm provides temporary relief
3. Shame about harming yourself
4. More emotional pain
5. Urge to self-harm again

**You're medicating the symptom, not treating the pain.**

### Alternative Coping (When Urges Hit)

**When you want to self-harm, try these first (even one):**

**Intense physical sensation (without injury):**
- Hold ice cubes in your hand until they melt
- Take a very cold/very hot shower
- Splash cold water on your face
- Run your hands under cold water
- Bite into a lemon

**Movement:**
- Run or sprint (tire your body)
- Do push-ups or intense exercise
- Dance intensely
- Punch a pillow repeatedly

**Sensory:**
- Listen to loud music that matches your mood
- Draw on your skin with marker (red or black)
- Rip paper instead of skin
- Squeeze a stress ball hard

**Emotional release:**
- Scream into a pillow
- Cry (let it out)
- Write angry letters (don't send)
- Punch a mattress

**Distraction:**
- Call someone (anyone)
- Watch an intense show/movie
- Play a game
- Do something that requires focus

**Grounding:**
- 5 things you see, 4 you touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste
- Hold ice and name what you feel
- Focus on your breathing

**No shame if you relapse.** Self-harm is an addiction. Relapses happen. Keep trying alternatives. Each time you don't self-harm is a win, even if you harmed yesterday.

## The Crisis Moment: First 24 Hours

### Right Now (This Hour)

**Do one of these:**
- [ ] Call 988 or Crisis Text Line
- [ ] Tell someone you trust (parent, friend, teacher, doctor)
- [ ] Go to an emergency room
- [ ] Call a trusted adult

**Don't:**
- ❌ Be alone
- ❌ Have access to means (remove sharps, pills, anything you might use)
- ❌ Isolate
- ❌ Make decisions about dying

### Today

- [ ] Tell someone (requirement—you need support)
- [ ] Go to ER if having active urges
- [ ] Remove access to self-harm items
- [ ] Keep crisis numbers visible
- [ ] Do one alternative coping when urges hit
- [ ] Tell someone who can check on you tomorrow

### This Week

- [ ] Schedule therapy (emergency appointment)
- [ ] See a psychiatrist (consider medication)
- [ ] Tell people in your life (you need support network)
- [ ] Create a safety plan (see below)
- [ ] Identify triggers (what led to this)
- [ ] Make crisis plan (who to call, where to go)

## Create a Safety Plan

### Step 1: Warning Signs
What happens before you get to crisis?
- Isolating
- Not sleeping
- Increased substance use
- Intrusive thoughts getting worse
- Loss of hope

**When you notice these, it's time to act.**

### Step 2: Internal Coping Strategies
(Things you can do alone)
- Breathing exercises
- Grounding techniques
- Listening to specific songs
- Looking at meaningful photos
- Journaling

### Step 3: People/Social Settings
Places and people that help:
- Friend's house
- Coffee shop (public, structured)
- Family member who gets it
- Support group
- Therapy office

### Step 4: Professional Resources
- Therapist: [name, number]
- Psychiatrist: [name, number]
- ER: [location, phone]
- Crisis line: 988
- Your doctor: [name, number]

### Step 5: Reasons to Live
Write these down (read when in crisis):
- People who would be devastated
- Future things you want to experience
- Accomplishments you're proud of
- Values that matter to you
- Dreams you haven't achieved

**Write this down. Put it somewhere you'll see it.**

## After the Crisis Moment Passes

### You're Not Out of Danger Yet

Crisis moments often come in waves:
- Day 1: Acute crisis
- Day 2-3: Exhausted but safer
- Day 4-7: Vulnerable, might spiral again
- Week 2+: Slowly improving

### What Helps Long-Term

**Therapy (essential):**
- Find therapist trained in crisis/suicide
- Be honest about everything
- Therapy might feel uncomfortable but it helps

**Medication (often needed):**
- Antidepressants can save your life
- Finding the right one takes time
- Be patient with the process
- Tell doctor if side effects are bad

**Support system (critical):**
- People who know and check on you
- Not isolation (isolation kills)
- Vulnerability (telling people you're struggling)
- Regular connection (calls, texts, in-person)

**Processing the pain:**
- Understanding what led to this
- Addressing the root (grief, trauma, loss, shame)
- Building better coping tools
- Learning to tolerate pain without harming

**Small goals:**
- Don't aim for "happy"
- Aim for "alive"
- Then "functional"
- Then "okay"
- Healing is slow but it happens

## Red Flags: When to Go to ER

**Go to emergency room if:**
- You have a plan to hurt yourself (with means)
- You're hearing voices telling you to hurt yourself
- You can't keep yourself safe
- You've already attempted or self-harmed
- You're about to lose control
- You don't feel safe being alone
- You're intoxicated and having thoughts
- You can't stop the thoughts even with crisis support

**It's okay to go.** People go to ERs for mental health crises all the time. You'll be safe. They'll help.

## When Someone You Know Is in Crisis

**If someone tells you they're suicidal:**

**Do:**
- ✅ Take it seriously (don't dismiss)
- ✅ Listen without judgment
- ✅ Believe them
- ✅ Help them get professional help NOW
- ✅ Call 988 or ER together
- ✅ Don't leave them alone
- ✅ Tell a trusted adult (teacher, parent, counselor)

**Don't:**
- ❌ Minimize ("You'll feel better soon")
- ❌ Debate whether they "should" die
- ❌ Promise to keep it secret
- ❌ Shame them
- ❌ Leave them alone
- ❌ Act like you can handle it without professional help

**Tell them:** "I'm glad you told me. You're not alone. Let's get help."
