# Financial Abuse: Recognition and Escape

## What is Financial Abuse?

**Financial abuse is using money and financial control to dominate, control, or isolate someone.**

It's one of the most common forms of abuse in relationships, and it's often invisible to outsiders.

**Examples:**
- Controlling all money
- Preventing you from working
- Running up debt in your name
- Stealing from you
- Forcing you to account for every dollar
- Using money as reward/punishment
- Sabotaging your job
- Making you financially dependent
- Hiding assets

**This is abuse.** It's not about being frugal or having different money values.

---

## Signs You're Being Financially Abused

### Control and Isolation

- Your partner controls all finances
- You ask permission to spend money
- You don't know what accounts exist
- You're not allowed to work (or they sabotage your job)
- You're isolated from family/friends (costs money to see them)
- You can't leave because you have no money

### Debt and Credit

- Debt in your name that you didn't authorize
- Credit cards opened in your name
- You're responsible for their debts
- Your credit is ruined (limiting your options)

### Economic Dependence

- You have no income
- You're completely dependent on them
- You can't afford to leave
- They use this as leverage ("I own you")

### Hidden Finances

- Secret accounts (you don't know about money)
- Refusing to discuss finances
- Lying about financial situation
- Hidden assets

### Money as Weapon

- Using money to punish (withholding it)
- Using money to reward (conditional support)
- Threatening to leave you with nothing
- Threatening to take kids (you can't afford custody fight)

### Sabotage

- Making you late to work (you get fired)
- Hiding job opportunities
- Not childcare so you can't work
- Spreading rumors at your workplace
- Threatening to report you for things

---

## Why Financial Abuse is Especially Trapping

**Unlike physical abuse, which has visible marks, financial abuse is:**
- Hard to prove
- Considered "private family business"
- Often rationalized ("I'm managing money for us")
- Making you believe you NEED them
- Making leaving impossible (no money, nowhere to go)

**It's psychological control with economic weapons.**

---

## The Cycle of Financial Abuse

**1. Control:** They limit your access to money

**2. Isolation:** You can't afford to leave or see others

**3. Dependence:** You believe you need them

**4. Trap:** You feel powerless to escape

**5. Abuse continues:** Because you feel stuck

## How to Safely Escape

### Phase 1: Secret Preparation (Before You Leave)

**Do this without them knowing:**

**1. Create a secret bank account**
- Use a bank they don't know about
- Open online (mail to trusted friend's address)
- Start depositing small amounts (cash, direct deposit if possible)
- Goal: Escape fund ($2,000-5,000 minimum)

**2. Gather documents (copies, hidden safely)**
- Birth certificate
- Social Security card
- Driver's license
- Passport
- Bank statements
- Proof of income
- Lease/mortgage documents
- Insurance policies
- Kids' documents (if applicable)
- Evidence of abuse (texts, emails, photos)

**3. Get a job (if you don't have one)**
- Tells you have independent income
- Gives you reason to leave house
- Builds confidence
- Creates escape fund

**4. Plan logistics**
- Where will you go? (friend's house, shelter, family)
- How will you get there? (car, public transit, friend)
- What will you take? (plan to grab quickly)
- When is safest? (when they're not home, or away for few days)

**5. Build support network**
- Tell trusted friend/family
- Ask for help when you leave
- Create code word (signal you need help)
- Plan for phone safety (they track it?)

**6. Document the abuse**
- Keep a journal (hidden) with dates/incidents
- Save threatening texts/emails
- Take photos of financial documents
- Record their statements (legal in one-party states)
- This helps with custody, divorce, protective order

### Phase 2: The Exit

**When you're ready to leave:**

**Do:**
- [ ] Leave when safe (they're not home or less likely to get angry)
- [ ] Take your documents
- [ ] Take only what fits in a bag/car
- [ ] Go to your safe place (friend, family, shelter)
- [ ] Tell someone you're leaving
- [ ] Block them on phone/social media
- [ ] Don't tell them where you are

**Don't:**
- ❌ Tell them you're leaving (they may escalate)
- ❌ Take anything that would make them violent
- ❌ Leave without your documents
- ❌ Go to a place they know about
- ❌ Keep your phone on them (they can track)

### Phase 3: After You Leave

**Immediate (first week):**
- [ ] Go to police if threatened
- [ ] Get protective order (if you feel unsafe)
- [ ] Change passwords on all accounts
- [ ] Cancel joint credit cards
- [ ] Open separate accounts (they can't access)
- [ ] Change phone number
- [ ] Tell your employer (they may come looking)

**First month:**
- [ ] Meet with divorce/family lawyer (many free consultations)
- [ ] Document all abuse (for custody/divorce)
- [ ] File for divorce/separation if applicable
- [ ] Open your own bank accounts
- [ ] Find stable housing
- [ ] Get job if you don't have one
- [ ] Apply for benefits if needed (TANF, food stamps, etc.)

**Ongoing:**
- [ ] Build financial independence
- [ ] Separate all finances
- [ ] Rebuild credit (check credit report)
- [ ] Therapy/counseling for trauma
- [ ] Support group (others who escaped)

## Financial Resources Available

### Free/Low-Cost Help

**Domestic Violence Shelters:**
- Provide housing, legal help, job training
- National Domestic Violence Hotline: 1-800-799-7233
- https://www.thehotline.org/

**Legal Aid:**
- Free legal help for low-income (Legal Aid Society)
- Divorce help at reduced cost
- Protective order assistance

**Financial Assistance:**
- TANF (Temporary Assistance for Needy Families)
- SNAP (food assistance)
- WIC (if you have children)
- Utility assistance programs
- Childcare assistance

**Job Training:**
- Many shelters offer job training
- Community colleges (financial aid available)
- Workforce development programs

**Financial Counseling:**
- Credit counseling (NFCC.org)
- Budgeting help
- Often free or low-cost

## Special Situations

### If You Have Children

**Your options:**
- Leave with children (document why)
- File for custody immediately
- Document their financial irresponsibility (if relevant)
- Get child support order
- Get protective order (covers kids too)

**Custody considerations:**
- Abusers often use custody as control
- Document their abusive behavior
- Show children are safer with you
- Get protective order naming children
- Work with family law attorney

**Resources for parents:**
- Legal aid (custody help)
- DV shelter (family services)
- Child protective services may help if abuse affects kids

### If You're on Their Insurance

**Plan ahead:**
- Research ACA marketplace (healthcare.gov)
- Apply before you leave (you'll lose coverage)
- Some states expand Medicaid
- Emergency Medicaid available

### If You Don't Have Income

**Building financial independence:**
- Get a job (even part-time)
- Apply for government assistance
- DV shelter may provide job training
- Build credit (difficult but possible)
- Live very simply until stable

## After You've Left: Rebuilding

### Immediate Priorities (First 3 Months)

1. **Safety** - Are you actually safe? Do you need protective order?
2. **Housing** - Do you have stable place to live?
3. **Food** - Can you feed yourself? Apply for SNAP if needed
4. **Job** - Do you have income or income plan?

### Short-Term (3-12 Months)

1. **Legal separation/divorce** - Work with lawyer
2. **Child support** - If applicable
3. **Credit repair** - Dispute fraudulent accounts
4. **Job stability** - Build employment history
5. **Therapy** - Processing abuse trauma

### Long-Term (Year 2+)

1. **Financial independence** - Own accounts, credit, savings
2. **Housing stability** - Permanent housing
3. **Career building** - Better job/skills
4. **Healing** - Therapy, support groups
5. **New relationships** - Only when ready, with healthy people

## Warning Signs You Might Go Back

**Abusers often:**
- Apologize profusely ("I've changed")
- Blame circumstances ("I was stressed")
- Promise things will be different
- Threaten self-harm ("I'll hurt myself")
- Threaten to hurt you
- Threaten custody ("I'll take the kids")
- Offer financial support ("I'll help you")

**If you're tempted to go back:**
- Remember why you left (reread your journal)
- Call DV hotline (they understand the pull)
- Talk to someone (don't isolate)
- Remember: abusers don't change without years of therapy
- Going back usually means abuse escalates

**You've already done the hardest part (leaving). Don't go back.**

---

## Red Flags: If You're in Danger

**Call 911 or DV hotline if:**
- They threaten you
- They show up where you are
- They try to access your money
- They try to take your children
- You feel unsafe for any reason

**National Domestic Violence Hotline: 1-800-799-7233**
- Free, confidential, 24/7
- Can help with safety planning
- Can connect to local resources

## Remember

1. **Financial abuse is real abuse.** It's not about money; it's about control.

2. **You can escape.** It takes planning, but you're not trapped forever.

3. **You deserve financial independence.** Your money is yours.

4. **Help exists.** Shelters, legal aid, government programs all exist to help.

5. **You're not alone.** Millions have escaped financial abuse. You can too.

6. **Your life will be better.** Hard now, but freedom is worth it.
