# Managing Financial Anxiety: Practical Strategies

## Understanding Financial Anxiety

Financial anxiety is the stress, worry, or fear that comes from concerns about money. It's one of the most common sources of stress and can affect sleep, relationships, and physical health.

**Common triggers:**
- Debt (credit cards, loans, medical bills)
- Job insecurity or irregular income
- Unexpected expenses
- Feeling behind compared to others
- Not knowing where your money goes
- Fear of the future

## Why We Feel Anxious About Money

Financial anxiety is real because:
1. **Money affects survival** - Housing, food, healthcare depend on it
2. **It triggers shame** - We learn money is "private" and feel embarrassed asking for help
3. **It's often taboo** - We rarely talk about it, so anxiety grows
4. **Uncertainty is stressful** - Not knowing the full picture makes it worse

**First truth:** If you're anxious about money, you're not alone. Most people are.

## Step 1: Face the Numbers (Even if It's Scary)

The first step is the hardest: knowing exactly what you owe and earn.

### Calculate Your Total Income (Monthly)

- Your salary/wages
- Side income
- Support from others
- Government assistance
- Other sources

**Monthly total: $_______**

### List Everything You Owe

Create a complete list:

| What you owe | Current balance | Monthly payment | Interest rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Credit card 1 | $____ | $____ | __% |
| Credit card 2 | $____ | $____ | __% |
| Student loans | $____ | $____ | __% |
| Personal loans | $____ | $____ | __% |
| Medical debt | $____ | $____ | __% |
| **Total debt** | **$____** | **$____** | |

**Important:** Write this down. Not knowing is more stressful than knowing.

### Track Monthly Expenses

For one month, write down every expense:

- Housing (rent, utilities, insurance)
- Food
- Transportation
- Phone/internet
- Subscriptions
- Personal care
- Entertainment
- Miscellaneous

**Question:** Where does money actually go?

## Step 2: Separate Facts from Feelings

Once you have the numbers:

**Facts (unchangeable right now):**
- "I currently owe $8,000"
- "My income is $2,500/month"
- "My expenses are $2,400/month"

**Feelings about those facts:**
- "I feel ashamed I have debt"
- "I feel scared I won't recover"
- "I feel stupid for getting into this"

**Key insight:** Facts are neutral. Your feelings about them are valid, but they're separate.

You can't change yesterday's debt, but you can change:
- How you think about it
- What you do tomorrow
- Your future choices

## Step 3: Create a Realistic Budget

You don't need a perfect budget—you need one you'll actually follow.

### Track what you ACTUALLY spend

Not what you think you should spend. For one month, write it down.

### Categorize it

- **Must-haves** (housing, food, utilities, medication): $____
- **Debt payments** (minimum payments): $____
- **Everything else** (entertainment, subscriptions, extras): $____

### Find the smallest cut

Don't cut everything at once. Pick ONE thing to reduce:
- Cancel one subscription ($10-15/month)
- Pack lunch 2 days instead of buying ($40-60/month)
- Reduce coffee out ($30/month)

Small cuts are sustainable.

### Create a simple system

You don't need an app. A notebook works:
- Write what you spend daily
- Add it up weekly
- Compare to your budget

## Step 4: Make a Debt Payoff Plan

### Option 1: Snowball Method (psychological win)
Pay off smallest debt first, then roll that payment into the next.
- **Pro:** Quick wins feel good, builds momentum
- **Con:** Costs more in interest if high-rate debt

### Option 2: Avalanche Method (saves money)
Pay minimums on all debt, attack highest interest rate first.
- **Pro:** Saves money overall
- **Con:** Takes longer to see results

### Option 3: Balanced Approach
- Pay minimums on everything
- Put extra money toward highest interest rate
- Celebrate when you pay off one card completely

**Pick one and commit.** Progress matters more than perfection.

## Step 5: Address the Anxiety (Not Just the Debt)

Even with a plan, anxiety can be overwhelming. These help:

### Separate "Today's Problem" from "Future's Problem"

**Today's problem:** "I can't pay the full balance"
**Solution:** Make the minimum payment

**Future's problem:** "How will I pay off $8,000?"
**Solution:** This is your budget plan—but it's not urgent today

Anxiety tries to solve tomorrow's problems today. It's not helpful.

### Use Grounding Techniques When Anxiety Spikes

When you catch yourself catastrophizing:

1. **Name 5 things you can see**
2. **Name 4 things you can touch**
3. **Name 3 things you can hear**
4. **Name 2 things you can smell**
5. **Name 1 thing you can taste**

This interrupts the anxiety spiral.

### Reframe Money Thoughts

**Anxious thought:** "I'm so irresponsible with money"
**Reframe:** "I've had money challenges, and I'm learning how to manage better"

**Anxious thought:** "I'll never pay this off"
**Reframe:** "If I pay $200/month, I'll pay off $2,400/year. Let me see how long each debt actually takes"

### Practice the "So What?" Technique

**Anxiety:** "What if I can't pay this?"
**Answer:** "Then I'll contact them and negotiate"

**Anxiety:** "What if I have an emergency?"
**Answer:** "I'll prioritize—food and housing first—and handle it"

**Anxiety:** "What if everyone finds out?"
**Answer:** "Most people have financial struggles. It's actually common."

## Step 6: Build a Support System

### Talk to Someone

Money shame keeps us isolated. Talking helps:
- Friend or family member
- Financial counselor (often free: NFCC.org)
- Therapist
- Support group (some are free/online)

### Get Free Financial Help

- **National Foundation for Credit Counseling** (NFCC.org) - free counselors
- **Federal Student Aid** - if you have student loans
- **Credit karma** - free credit monitoring
- **Community banks** - often have free financial literacy classes

### Join Online Communities

- Reddit: r/personalfinance, r/debtfree
- Facebook groups on budgeting
- Support groups (many free online)

## Reality Check

**Paying off debt takes time.** If you have $8,000 in debt and pay $200/month, that's about 40 months (3+ years). That's normal and okay.

**Progress is progress.** Paying $100 this month is better than being paralyzed by anxiety.

**You're not broken.** Financial stress is common. The fact that you're reading this means you care enough to improve.

---

## When to Seek Professional Help

Talk to a financial counselor or therapist if:
- Your anxiety is preventing you from taking action
- You're avoiding bills because you're scared
- Money stress is affecting your sleep or health
- You're considering risky financial decisions
- You feel hopeless about your situation

**Free crisis resources:**
- Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741
- SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357
