# Breakup: First Steps After the Relationship Ends

## The First 24 Hours (Survival Mode)

Right after a breakup, your brain is in shock. Your only job is to survive the day. You don't have to be strong, wise, or rational. You just have to get through.

### What's Happening in Your Body

- Adrenaline surge (racing heart, shaking)
- Emotional numbness mixed with intense pain
- Inability to think clearly
- Physical symptoms: nausea, headache, chest tightness
- Sleep disrupted or you want to sleep to escape

**This is normal. Your nervous system is overwhelmed.**

### Don't Do These Things (First 24 Hours)

❌ Contact them (text, call, show up)
❌ Post on social media
❌ Delete their number (you will)
❌ Delete photos (yet)
❌ Drink heavily to cope
❌ Make major decisions
❌ Isolate completely

### Do These Things Instead

✅ **Tell someone you trust** - Call, text, or go to someone's house. You shouldn't be alone.

✅ **Let yourself feel** - Cry, scream, be angry. Don't suppress it.

✅ **Basic self-care only:**
- Drink water
- Eat something (even if you're not hungry)
- Shower (warm water helps)
- Change clothes
- Try to sleep (sleep aids okay if needed)

✅ **Create a barrier to contact:**
- Give your phone to a trusted friend
- Log out of social media
- Leave your house if needed
- Block them (yes, immediately—not to be mean, to protect you)

✅ **Move your body:**
- Walk around the block
- Stretch
- Do jumping jacks
- Anything to burn off nervous energy

✅ **Limit decisions:**
- What's for dinner? (no decision—order something)
- What to watch? (something familiar, not triggering)
- Should you respond? (No. Not today.)

## Days 2-7: The Crash

After the initial shock, the sadness hits. This is when it feels worst.

### Why Days 3-5 Are the Hardest

- Adrenaline wears off
- Reality sets in
- No adrenaline to numb the pain
- You're exhausted
- You wake up and remember they're gone (multiple times a day)

### Managing the First Week

**Have a "hard moments" plan:**

When you want to contact them:
- Call your support person instead
- Go for a walk
- Do something physical
- Wait 20 minutes (urge will pass)

**When you can't sleep at 3am:**
- Don't text them (this is the urge talking)
- Listen to a podcast or audiobook
- Read (not about breakups or relationships)
- Guided meditation
- Write in a journal (don't send to them)

**When you see something that reminds you of them:**
- Let yourself feel sad for 5 minutes
- Then redirect (change song, scroll away, leave the location)
- You're retraining your brain to not react

### Practical First Week Tasks

**If you lived together:**
- [ ] Decide who moves out (immediately)
- [ ] Divide belongings (quickly, not perfectly)
- [ ] Get their stuff back to them (through a friend if needed)
- [ ] Change the locks if necessary

**Mutual friends/connections:**
- [ ] Tell close friends (they'll find out anyway)
- [ ] Don't bad-mouth them (yet—that comes later)
- [ ] Ask friends not to share updates about them
- [ ] Consider muting them on social media

**Practical stuff:**
- [ ] Change passwords (if they know them)
- [ ] Update emergency contacts
- [ ] Cancel shared subscriptions
- [ ] Update relationship status (if comfortable—or leave it)

**Financial:**
- [ ] Stop any automatic payments to them
- [ ] Update insurance/benefits
- [ ] Separate bank accounts if needed
- [ ] Collect any money they owe you (ask politely once, then drop it)

## The Second Week: Temptation

Week 2 is when the temptation to contact them is strongest. The pain is still intense, but you're functional enough to act on it.

### The Urge to Contact Them

**You'll want to text/call because:**
- "I miss them" (true, but contacting won't help)
- "I just want to explain" (they won't understand)
- "Maybe I made a mistake" (pain talking)
- "I saw something funny, I want to share" (hardest urge)
- "I hope they're okay" (they are)
- "I want closure" (you get closure through healing, not contact)

**When the urge hits:**
1. **Acknowledge it:** "I want to text them. That's normal."
2. **Wait:** Set a timer for 20 minutes. Urges pass.
3. **Redirect:** Text your support person instead.
4. **Remember:** Contacting = Starting the pain over. Not worth it.

### Making Contact Harder

**Block them on:**
- Text/call
- Social media (all platforms)
- Email (archive their emails, don't read them)
- Dating apps (if relevant)

**Why blocking is healthy:**
- Not punishment; it's protection
- You're protecting future-you from weak moments
- When you're tempted at 2am, you can't easily unblock
- It forces you to sit with the pain (which heals you)

**Tell your support people:**
- "I'm likely to ask you to unblock them. Please say no."
- Accountability helps

## Week 3+: The "Am I Doing This Right?" Phase

By week 3, you're managing better but second-guessing everything.

### Common Thoughts

**"Maybe I should text them"**
- No. This thought comes every few days for weeks. It doesn't mean you made a mistake.

**"Maybe we'll get back together"**
- Possible, but build your life as if you won't. If you reunite, great. If not, you're already healing.

**"Should I have done things differently?"**
- Rumination is normal. Limit it to 10 minutes/day, then stop.

**"Are they doing okay?"**
- They are. People are resilient. You can't fix them, and it's not your job anymore.

**"What if no one else wants me?"**
- You will meet people again. This doesn't feel true now, but it will.

### Building the New Routine

By week 3, create a new routine that doesn't include them:

**Morning:**
- Wake up at consistent time
- Exercise or walk (helps with mood)
- Breakfast
- Work/school

**Afternoon:**
- Stick to responsibilities
- See a friend if possible
- Do something you enjoy (hobby, project)
- Avoid free time (when rumination hits)

**Evening:**
- No social media check-ins on them
- Dinner
- Hobby, show, book (not relationship drama)
- Bed at reasonable time

**Why routine matters:**
- Gives structure to shapeless grief
- Prevents obsessive thinking
- Starts rebuilding your identity
- Physical activity helps mood

## Red Flags: When You Need Extra Support

**Consider therapy if:**
- You're not eating/sleeping after 2 weeks
- You're having thoughts of self-harm
- You're unable to function at work/school
- You're using substances to cope
- You're in contact with them repeatedly (can't stop)
- Depression is deepening (not improving after week 3-4)

**Crisis:**
- 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline
- Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741

## The "Do I Reach Out?" Question

**Common scenarios:**

**"They want to be friends"**
- Not right now. Friendship requires emotional distance you don't have yet.
- Revisit in 6-12 months after you've healed.

**"They're going through something"**
- You care, but it's not your job. Their support system can help.
- If you respond, you're restarting your healing.

**"I left something at their place"**
- Get a friend to retrieve it. Don't go yourself.
- If it's not replaceable, ask them to mail it (don't negotiate).

**"I just want to say goodbye properly"**
- You already did. Goodbye was the breakup.
- Another conversation won't bring closure.

**"I want to make sure they know I'm sorry"**
- If you did something that hurt them, one honest, brief apology is okay.
- Then stop. Don't wait for forgiveness or response.

---

## What Contact Does

**Every time you contact them:**
- You restart your healing clock (back to day 1)
- You give them power over your pain
- You confuse them (mixed signals)
- You confuse yourself
- You delay accepting it's over
- You extend the agony

**It might feel like:**
- "At least I did something"
- "At least I got a response"
- "At least we're still talking"

**But it actually:**
- Prevents moving forward
- Reopens the wound
- Keeps you stuck
- Makes healing take 2-3x longer

## The First Month Checklist

- [ ] Blocked on all platforms (phone, social media, email)
- [ ] Their stuff returned or arranged
- [ ] Your stuff retrieved (through a friend if needed)
- [ ] Support system activated (friends, family, therapist)
- [ ] New routine established
- [ ] Haven't contacted them (even once)
- [ ] Are eating, sleeping, moving your body
- [ ] Have at least one thing to look forward to each week
- [ ] Starting to see glimpses of hours where you don't think about them

---

## Healing Timeline Expectations

- **Week 1:** Shock, pain, can't believe it's real
- **Week 2-3:** Worst pain, urges to contact strongest
- **Week 4-8:** Pain is still heavy, but you have good hours mixed in
- **Month 3:** You have good days, not just good hours
- **Month 6:** You can think about them without immediate pain
- **Month 12:** You've integrated the loss, you're building new identity

**This timeline varies.** Some people heal faster; some need longer. Both are okay.

---

## Remember

1. **Don't contact them.** Not to explain, not to say hi, not for closure. It will set you back.

2. **Block them everywhere.** Not as punishment—as self-protection.

3. **Tell your support system.** Ask them to hold you accountable.

4. **The urge to contact passes.** It doesn't mean you should do it.

5. **You will feel normal again.** Not today, not this week. But you will.
