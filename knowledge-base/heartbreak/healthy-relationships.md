# Healthy Relationships: What to Look For, What to Avoid

## What is a Healthy Relationship?

A healthy relationship is one where **both people feel safe, respected, and valued.** It's not about being perfect or never disagreeing—it's about how you treat each other when things get hard.

**Core elements:**
- Trust and honesty
- Respect for each other's boundaries
- Open communication
- Mutual support
- Individual identity (you're still "you")
- Conflict resolution without fear
- Equality in decision-making

---

## Green Flags: Signs of a Healthy Relationship

### Trust and Honesty
✓ You can be yourself without pretending
✓ They tell you the truth, even when it's hard
✓ You trust they're not hiding things
✓ You don't feel the need to check their phone or follow them
✓ You believe what they tell you

### Respect
✓ They listen to your opinions, even if they disagree
✓ They ask permission before doing things involving you
✓ They respect your boundaries
✓ They don't pressure you into anything
✓ They accept your "no" without argument
✓ They respect your family, friends, hobbies

### Communication
✓ You can talk about feelings without fear of anger
✓ They ask how you're doing and actually listen
✓ You can disagree without it becoming a fight
✓ You can say "I'm hurt" and they take it seriously
✓ They don't bring up old fights to win arguments
✓ You both try to understand, not just be right

### Support
✓ They celebrate your wins
✓ They support your goals and dreams
✓ They're there when you're struggling
✓ They don't try to change who you are
✓ They encourage your friendships and hobbies
✓ They help you be better, not smaller

### Equality
✓ Decisions are made together
✓ Neither person has all the power
✓ Both people's needs matter equally
✓ Money, chores, plans are discussed fairly
✓ No one is controlling the other
✓ Both people have agency

### Emotional Safety
✓ You don't walk on eggshells
✓ You don't fear their anger or judgment
✓ You can be vulnerable without shame
✓ They comfort you when you're upset
✓ You can express sadness, anger, or fear
✓ You feel accepted as you are

### Physical Safety
✓ Your body is respected
✓ They ask before touching you
✓ You feel safe alone with them
✓ They don't hurt you or threaten you
✓ They respect your sexual boundaries
✓ You're never afraid of them

## Red Flags: Warning Signs to Watch For

### Control and Manipulation
🚩 They get angry if you spend time with friends/family
🚩 They criticize what you wear, how you look, your interests
🚩 They check your phone or demand passwords
🚩 They make you feel guilty for having other relationships
🚩 They isolate you from people who care about you
🚩 They make big decisions without consulting you
🚩 They pressure you into sexual activity
🚩 They monitor where you go and who you're with
🚩 They use guilt: "If you loved me, you'd..."

### Dishonesty and Betrayal
🚩 You catch them in lies
🚩 They deny things you witnessed
🚩 They cheat or flirt inappropriately
🚩 They gaslight you ("That didn't happen," "You're crazy")
🚩 They keep secrets about important things
🚩 You can't trust what they tell you

### Disrespect
🚩 They put you down, mock you, or humiliate you
🚩 They make fun of your appearance, intelligence, or interests
🚩 They disrespect your family or friends
🚩 They roll their eyes when you talk
🚩 They interrupt constantly or don't listen
🚩 They treat you differently in public vs. private
🚩 They dismiss your feelings ("You're overreacting")

### Anger and Aggression
🚩 They have explosive anger over small things
🚩 They yell, scream, or throw things
🚩 They punch walls, slam doors, or break things
🚩 They threaten you
🚩 They push, grab, or hit you
🚩 They intimidate you with anger
🚩 They don't apologize for losing their temper

### Emotional Abuse
🚩 You feel scared, anxious, or walking on eggshells
🚩 They withdraw affection as punishment
🚩 They threaten to leave or hurt themselves
🚩 They make you feel unlovable or not good enough
🚩 They use your insecurities against you
🚩 They apologize, then do it again
🚩 You feel like you're always wrong

### Isolation
🚩 They don't want you to have other relationships
🚩 Your friends and family say they're worried
🚩 You've lost contact with important people
🚩 You feel like they're your only source of support
🚩 They discourage your hobbies or interests
🚩 You only feel comfortable with them

### Financial Control
🚩 They control the money
🚩 They won't let you work or go to school
🚩 They take your money or make you account for spending
🚩 You don't have access to resources
🚩 You're dependent on them financially

## The Cycle of Abuse (If You're Seeing Red Flags)

**Many unhealthy relationships follow a pattern:**

1. **Tension building** - They're irritable, critical, controlling
2. **Incident** - Anger, yelling, accusations, sometimes physical
3. **Reconciliation** - "I'm sorry, I love you, it won't happen again"
4. **Honeymoon period** - They're extra nice, loving, attentive
5. **Repeat** - Back to tension building

**Each cycle may take days, weeks, or months. The pattern gets worse over time.**

If you see this cycle, **that's a sign the relationship is unhealthy.**

## Healthy vs. Unhealthy Arguments

### Unhealthy Arguments
❌ Name-calling or insults
❌ Bringing up old fights
❌ Yelling to be heard
❌ Refusing to listen
❌ Making it about "winning"
❌ Bringing up someone's trauma or insecurities as weapons
❌ Threatening to leave or hurt yourself
❌ Physical aggression (throwing, hitting, pushing)

### Healthy Arguments
✓ You both stay calm(ish)
✓ You listen to understand, not to win
✓ You use "I feel" statements
✓ You focus on the current issue
✓ You care about their perspective
✓ You're willing to compromise
✓ You take breaks if emotions are too high
✓ You work toward a solution together
✓ You apologize when you're wrong
✓ You move forward (not bringing it up constantly)

## Setting Healthy Boundaries

Boundaries are **limits on what you will and won't accept** in a relationship.

### Examples of Healthy Boundaries

**About communication:**
- "I'm not available to text during work"
- "I need 24 hours notice before plans"
- "I won't argue over text; let's talk in person"

**About alone time:**
- "I need time with friends every week"
- "I need alone time to recharge"
- "I want one night a week to myself"

**About your body:**
- "I'm not ready to have sex"
- "I don't want to be touched when I'm upset"
- "I'm not comfortable with PDA"

**About honesty:**
- "I need complete honesty about serious things"
- "I need to know if you're talking to your ex"

**About other relationships:**
- "My friendships are important and non-negotiable"
- "I need to stay close to my family"

### How to Set a Boundary

**Step 1: Be clear**
"I feel uncomfortable when you go through my phone. I need privacy."

**Step 2: Explain why (optional)**
"It makes me feel like you don't trust me."

**Step 3: State the consequence**
"If this continues, I won't be able to stay in this relationship."

**Step 4: Follow through**
If they cross the boundary again, actually follow through.

### Red Flag Response to Boundaries
- They get angry
- They guilt trip you
- They claim you don't love them
- They ignore the boundary
- They push back repeatedly

**Healthy people respect boundaries. Period.**

## What Happens After a Breakup in a Healthy Relationship

**Even healthy relationships end sometimes.** When they do:

✓ You both accept it (even if sad)
✓ You communicate about it clearly
✓ You can still respect each other
✓ You don't become enemies
✓ You can maintain a friendship (if you both want)
✓ You don't badmouth them to others
✓ You take time to heal
✓ You learn from it for next time

**In unhealthy relationships:**
❌ They won't accept the breakup
❌ They threaten or beg
❌ They stalk or harass you
❌ They badmouth you to everyone
❌ You feel relief (not sadness)
❌ You have to go no-contact to feel safe

## Questions to Ask Yourself

**About your current or past relationships:**

- Do I feel safe with this person?
- Can I be fully myself?
- Do they respect my boundaries?
- Can I trust them?
- Do I feel supported?
- Am I afraid of their reaction to things?
- Do I have time for my friends and family?
- Do I feel better or worse about myself?
- Can we resolve conflicts respectfully?
- Is this relationship adding to my life or draining it?

**If you answered "no" to more than 2, this might be an unhealthy relationship.**

## What Healthy Love Feels Like

Healthy love:
- Feels safe and calm (not chaotic)
- Allows you to grow
- Respects who you are
- Feels mutual and equal
- Includes disagreements handled respectfully
- Makes you feel better about yourself
- Lets you be yourself fully
- Involves trust
- Is sustainable (not exhausting)

## If You're in an Unhealthy Relationship

### Safety First
If you feel unsafe:
- Call 911 if in immediate danger
- Go to a safe place
- Tell someone you trust

### Get Support
- Talk to a trusted person
- Call the National Domestic Violence Hotline: 1-800-799-7233 (24/7, confidential)
- Text START to 88788
- Visit thehotline.org for resources

### Make a Plan
- You might not be ready to leave immediately (that's normal)
- But start planning: Save money, tell a trusted person, know where you'll go
- Talk to a counselor about your options

### Resources
- **National Domestic Violence Hotline:** 1-800-799-7233
- **Crisis Text Line:** Text HOME to 741741
- **RAINN (sexual assault):** 1-800-656-4673

## Remember

1. **You deserve respect.** Always.

2. **Red flags don't improve with love.** People must want to change.

3. **Leaving is brave,** not weak or ungrateful.

4. **Your gut instinct matters.** If something feels off, it probably is.

5. **You're worth the effort.** A healthy relationship shouldn't require you to shrink yourself.
